# Competitive landscape: digital wardrobe, outfit planning, AI stylist and weather-to-outfit apps (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: 21 tool calls (web search + fetch). Google Play pages could not be fetched directly (content truncated), so store figures come from search snippets / AppBrain and may vary by locale and date. Several comparison pages are written by competing vendors (GetWardrobe, Portal, PutTogether, Fits, Clother), so treat their claims as vendor-authored. Every figure below is tagged with its source; anything without a source is in Gaps.

## 1. Competitor profiles: launch, country, funding, traction, business model, features

### Takeaway
The category is crowded and well funded at the top: Whering (UK, ~$14M total, 10M+ users), Alta (US, $11M seed in 2025 + $20M Series A in Oct 2026), Daydream (US, $50M seed), and Acloset (Korea, 5M+ Play downloads, 8M+ users claimed). Most others are small or bootstrapped. Business models are split between freemium subscriptions (Acloset, Indyx, Cladwell, Pureple, Fits), credit packs (Whering) and affiliate/commerce (Alta, Daydream).

## 2. Which competitors have weather-based suggestions and social features?

### Takeaway
Weather-aware suggestions are now table stakes among leaders (Whering, Acloset, Alta, Cladwell, Fits, GetWardrobe, Clueless, and most French apps). Social features exist in Whering, Acloset, Indyx, Fits, Pureple, GetWardrobe and Alta, but they are about sharing closets, letting friends style you, or community feeds, not joint planning.

### Cited Findings
- Weather: Whering (planner forecast, custom location per date), Acloset (forecast, date up to 7 days ahead), Indyx (today only), Fits (AI uses local weather), Alta, Clueless, Cladwell (AI uses weather), GetWardrobe (dual-source forecast, alerts); Stylebook and Pureple: none listed — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/)
- Whering: "the Planner now suggests outfits from your wardrobe based on the weather" (French listing) — [App Store FR](https://apps.apple.com/td/app/whering-garde-robe-virtuel/id1519461680?l=fr-FR)
- Social: Whering lets friends create outfits for you via "style submissions" and add friends' outfits to moodboards — [Whering App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/whering-your-digital-closet/id1519461680), [Whering blog "What's new on Whering social"](https://whering.co.uk/thoughts/whats-new-on-Whering-social)
- Indyx: get your closet styled by friends or other users — [Indyx App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/indyx-wardrobe-outfit-app/id1599179405)
- Fits: friends can view your clothes and make outfits for you depending on privacy settings — [Fits](https://www.fits-app.com/)
- Acloset: community to share style tips and "plan outfits with friends" — [Acloset Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.looko.acloset&hl=en_US)
- Alta: community feed of avatar looks with weekly Top 100; Pureple: users style each other's wardrobes; GetWardrobe: community feed + family sharing — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/)
- Weather "depth" (felt temperature, rain, morning-to-evening swing) is claimed lacking in Acloset and Cladwell by a vendor test — [PutTogether, 2026](https://www.puttogether.world/guides/digital-closet/wake-up-already-dressed-ai-outfit-app-weather-2026) (vendor-authored)

### Inferences
- Weather is no longer a differentiator; quality of weather logic (feels-like, rain, day-long swing, meeting-place forecast at a specific time) could still be.
- "Friends" features exist in about half the leaders, so a friends network alone won't stand out; combining social + weather + coordination might.

### Gaps
- Whether Acloset's "plan outfits with friends" means joint coordination or only sharing; the listing gives no detail.

## 3. Does any app already offer coordinated / matching outfits between two or more people?

### Takeaway
I found no wardrobe app that automatically computes color-coordinated outfits for two or more people from their own wardrobes, let alone using the weather at a meeting place. Adjacent products exist: (a) event apps where guests share planned outfits to avoid duplicates or match a vibe (aDress, Panrobe, a GitHub project "Fetch"); (b) AI image generators that render matching couple outfits (YouCam, Pippit, Resleeve, MyPixelMind, AIClothSwap); (c) "friends style me" features in Whering, Indyx, Fits, Pureple. The differentiator looks real, but it's a narrow one and the search wasn't exhaustive.

### Cited Findings
- aDress - Event Outfit Planning (iOS, developer Gavin Rudling / GJR Publishing, site adress.com.au): event galleries of what each guest plans to wear, coordination to "match the event's vibe" and avoid duplicates, comments, private galleries; free + $11.99 lifetime premium; no ratings yet; no automated color matching, no weather, no wardrobe — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6766782405)
- Panrobe: plan events, invite guests, group chat, share what you'll wear (idea came from duplicate wedding dresses) — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/panrobe)
- Fetch: open-source app where friends share what they'll wear to parties — [GitHub](https://github.com/sarahgrossman/fetch)
- WearThatApp: friends pick an outfit for you — [BetaList](https://betalist.com/startups/wearthatapp)
- AI couple-matching image generators (photo try-on, not wardrobe-based): YouCam Perfect "AI Fashion", Pippit couple templates, Resleeve, MyPixelMind (couples, BFFs, family, shared palette), AIClothSwap — [Perfect Corp](https://www.perfectcorp.com/consumer/blog/photo-editing/random-outfit-generator), [CyberLink](https://www.cyberlink.com/blog/ai-app-photo-editing/2610/how-to-generate-matching-outfits-for-couples-on-app), [Pippit](https://www.pippit.ai/templates/ai-couple-outfits), [Resleeve](https://resleeve.ai/blog/ai-powered-couple-matching-outfits-how-technology-is-evolving-couple-fashion), [MyPixelMind](https://www.mypixelmind.com/ai-matching-outfit-generator), [AIClothSwap](https://aiclothswap.com/outfits/couple-matching)
- Whering Social: friends style outfits for each other; submissions can be declined — [Whering blog](https://whering.co.uk/thoughts/whats-new-on-Whering-social)
- The vendor comparison of Whering, Indyx, Acloset, Alta, Style DNA and Portal describes no coordinated-outfit feature for couples, friends or groups — [Portal comparison, Jul 2026](https://portal-f4u.com/best-ai-stylist-apps/)
- Margot composes an outfit for a destination/occasion (restaurant, party, date) including weather, but for one user only — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/margot-dressing-virtuel/id6766047882?l=fr-FR)

### Inferences
- The "meetup" mode (two real wardrobes + color harmony + forecast at the meeting place and time) appears unoccupied as of Nov 2026. The closest threats are (1) Whering adding it to its existing friends graph, and (2) event apps like aDress adding wardrobe/AI. Incumbents could copy it quickly, so speed and execution matter more than the idea.
- Demand signal exists (couple/BFF/family matching content, "twinning" trends, AI couple generators), but it's mostly for photos and occasions, not daily meetups. The meetup use case needs validation.

### Gaps
- No exhaustive check of Asian markets (Korea/China/Japan couple-look culture, e.g., Korean "couple look" apps); Acloset is Korean and could plausibly add it. Not verified.
- Didn't check TikTok/Lemon8 native features or ChatGPT/Gemini prompts used informally for couple coordination.

## 4. Common complaints and praise in reviews

### Takeaway
Complaints cluster on: tedious item entry, free-tier item caps/paywalls (Acloset 100 items), crashes/bugs (Acloset, Combyne), weak AI suggestions, and server/shutdown worries (Cladwell). Praise goes to fast photo upload and tagging, background removal, cost-per-wear stats, outfit calendars/selfie logs, and a good free tier.

### Cited Findings
- Acloset: users complain about the 100-item free cap ("otherwise pay up"), regular crashes, weak AI outfit suggestions; praise fast photo uploading/tagging, "best Android wardrobe app" — [Acloset Google Play reviews / AppBrain](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.looko.acloset)
- Indyx: users very positive, one paid "without a second thought"; main friction is the time it takes to document a wardrobe — [Substack "To Indyx or not to Indyx" comments](https://nowardrobe.substack.com/p/to-indyx-or-not-to-indyx/comments), [Indyx vs Stylebook comments](https://rabbitfurcoat.substack.com/p/indyx-vs-stylebook/comments)
- Whering: praised for cost-per-wear stats and daily outfit photo logging / selfie calendar — [Substack comments](https://oferodrig.substack.com/p/what-i-learned-from-taking-daily/comments)
- Cladwell: undated App Store review says the app lost server connectivity and shut down — [AppSupports negative reviews](https://appsupports.co/1140550878/cladwell/negative-reviews) (single user comment, not confirmed)
- Combyne: recent Google Play reviews complain of bugs — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.combyne.app&hl=en)

### Inferences
- Onboarding cost (photographing 50-200 items) is the main barrier. A weather app that gives value on day 1 without a full wardrobe (generic weather outfit, then gradual wardrobe filling) helps retention.
- Item caps on free tiers create friction; a generous free tier (Whering, Indyx, Fits are unlimited) is now the norm.

### Gaps
- Couldn't retrieve Reddit threads (r/femalefashionadvice, r/capsulewardrobe) directly; no quantified complaint frequencies. App Store review text for Whering/Stylebook/Pureple was not fetched.

## 5. Failed, shut-down or pivoted apps and why

### Takeaway
Exits in this category are usually small: Finery's tech was sold to Stitch Fix and the app closed (2019); Cladwell hit a subscription growth ceiling and was sold to its co-founder (2019); Clueless stopped selling its standalone app (Nov 2026); Whering closed its secondhand marketplace (Aug 2024). Standalone wardrobe subscriptions struggle to scale.

### Cited Findings
- Finery: Stitch Fix acquired Finery's technology and IP (not the company); consumer app closed, users could download wardrobes until 6 Sep 2019 — [FashionUnited, Sep 2019](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/stitch-fix-buys-digital-wardrobe-service-finery/2019090329746), [WWD](https://wwd.com/business-news/technology/stitch-fix-finery-acquisition-1203250298/)
- Cladwell: founded 2011, raised $3.5-3.8M, peak subscription revenue nearly $1M, ~$330k ARR and ~10,000 paid subs at sale in Aug 2019 to Erin and Colin Flynn; reason: growth ceiling, unclear how to expand beyond subscriptions "in a way that was compelling to ... investors" — [TheyGotAcquired](https://theygotacquired.com/saas/cladwell-acquired/). Still operating in 2026 with Erin Flynn as CEO — [PutTogether 2026](https://www.puttogether.world/guides/digital-closet/stylebook-vs-cladwell-vs-whering-vs-puttogether-2026)
- Clueless (2024 app) stopped selling its standalone app in Nov 2026, moving into AI assistants — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/)
- Whering secondhand marketplace closed Aug 2024 — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/)
- Pureple rebranded as "AI outfit planner" in 2026 — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/)
- The Yes (Julie Bornstein) sold to Pinterest in 2022; Bornstein then started Daydream — [Fortune, Jun 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/06/25/daydream-fashion-ai-shopping-agent-marketplace-julie-bornstein/)

### Inferences
- Cladwell's numbers (~10k payers at peak ~$1M) are a realistic ceiling benchmark for a subscription-only wardrobe app; affiliate/commerce or B2B are the paths investors favour (Alta, Daydream, Whering + eBay).
- Clueless exiting standalone apps in favour of AI assistants signals a risk: general assistants (ChatGPT/Gemini) may absorb basic "what should I wear" queries.

### Gaps
- Save Your Wardrobe (UK) status/pivot not verified in this research. No confirmed Combyne shutdown.

## 6. How leading apps reduce the "adding clothes is tedious" problem

### Takeaway
Leaders combine automatic background removal with several bulk-import paths: email/receipt import (Acloset via Gmail for Amazon/Zara/Shein; Indyx forwarded receipts), product-link paste (GetWardrobe, Indyx), browser extension (Whering), camera-roll/outfit-photo scanning (Whering "gallery scanner", Acloset single mirror selfie), product-database matching (Whering, 100M+ products) and even human cataloguing services (Indyx).

### Cited Findings
- Automatic background removal in Acloset, Whering, Indyx, Clueless — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/)
- Acloset imports from Amazon, Zara, Shein via Gmail/receipts; detects garments from a single mirror selfie — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/), [Portal comparison](https://portal-f4u.com/best-ai-stylist-apps/)
- Indyx: product links and forwarded receipts; paid in-home cataloguing service in some US cities — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/), [Portal comparison](https://portal-f4u.com/best-ai-stylist-apps/)
- Whering: Chrome extension; "gallery scanner" extracting items from camera-roll outfit photos; auto-enhancing images to retailer quality; matching against 100M+ product database — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/), [FashionUnited, Jul 2026](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/whering-secures-funding-from-ebay-ventures-and-google-ai-futures/2026070773352), [Portal comparison](https://portal-f4u.com/best-ai-stylist-apps/)
- GetWardrobe: paste a shopping link to auto-fill details; screenshot upload — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/)
- Fits: "clip from web" — [GetWardrobe compare](https://getwardrobe.com/compare/)

### Inferences
- For this project, product-link import (already planned) matches the leaders; add on-device background removal (ML Kit subject segmentation on Android / Vision on iOS) and outfit-photo extraction to stay competitive. Receipt/email import is a strong differentiator but adds privacy/OAuth complexity (Gmail API restricted scopes).
- For French users, link import must handle FR retailers (Zara FR, Kiabi, Vinted, Galeries Lafayette, Shein FR) and €-pricing.

### Gaps
- No data on what share of items users add by each method, or retention impact of import features.
